<p>We study prophet inequalities for \(n\) i.i.d. base rewards subject to multiplicative discounts across \(S\) phases. For equal-length phases and a common discount factor \(\widetilde\gamma\), we characterize the optimal competitive ratio of single-quantile policies as \(n\to\infty\) with \(S\) and \(\widetilde\gamma\) fixed. The resulting sharp guarantee describes the dependence on the phase parameters: it recovers \(1-1/e\) without discounting and approaches \(1/2\) in the worst-case regime. We also establish a distribution-free comparison that bounds the discounted prophet by a fractional i.i.d. maximum with effective horizon \(B\), the sum of the discount weights across all observations. The comparison yields acceptance probability \(1/B\), determined only by the phase profile. This calibration attains the canonical optimum even among single-quantile policies that may choose their acceptance probability using the known base distribution. The comparison also gives guarantees for heterogeneous discounts and unequal phase lengths. A complementary optimal-stopping upper bound establishes the same limiting optimum of \(1/2\) for arbitrary stopping rules in the canonical model. Finally, the same effective-horizon calibration sets acceptance probability \(1-\widetilde\gamma\) under infinite-horizon geometric discounting. This explicit quantile rule attains Boshuizen's (1994) known optimal worst-case ratio \(1/(1+\widetilde\gamma)\) without solving a Bellman equation.</p>
