We introduce a bandit framework for stochastic matching under the multinomial logit (MNL) choice model. In this setting, N agents on one side are assigned to K arms on the other side, where each arm stochastically selects an agent from its assigned pool according to unknown preferences and yields a corresponding reward over a horizon T . The objective is to minimize regret by maximizing the cumulative revenue from successful matches. A naive approach requires solving an NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem at every round, resulting in a prohibitive computational cost. To address this challenge, we propose batched algorithms that strategically limit the number of times matching assignments are updated to Θ(log log T ) over the entire horizon. By invoking expensive combinatorial optimization only on a vanishing fraction of rounds, our algorithms substantially reduce overall computational overhead while still achieving a regret bound of O( √ T ).
