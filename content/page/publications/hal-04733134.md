<p>We study the preference-based pure exploration problem for bandits with vector-valued rewards ordered using a preference cone \(\mathcal{C}\) with the goal of identifying the most preferred policy over the set of arms. First, to quantify the impact of preferences, we derive a novel lower bound on the sample complexity for identifying the most preferred policy with confidence level \(1-\delta\). Our lower bound elicits the role played by the geometry of the preference cone and punctuates the difference in hardness compared to best-arm variants of the problem. We further explicate this geometry when rewards follow a Gaussian distributions, and provide a convex reformulation of the lower bound. Then, we leverage this convex reformulation of the lower bound to design the Preference-based Track and Stop (PreTS) algorithm that identifies the most preferred policy. Finally, we derive a new concentration result for vector-valued rewards, and show that PreTS achieves a matching sample complexity upper bound.</p>
