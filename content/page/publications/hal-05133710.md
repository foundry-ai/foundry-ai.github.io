<p>We show that for any \(ε&gt;0\), as the number of agents gets large, the share of games that admit a pure \(ε\)-equilibrium converges to 1. Our result holds even for pure \(ε\)-equilibrium in which all agents, except for at most one, play a best response. In contrast, it is known that the share of games that admit a pure Nash equilibrium, that is, for \(ε=0\), is asymptotically \(1-1/e\approx 0.63\). This suggests that very small deviations from perfect rationality, captured by positive values of \(ε\), suffice to ensure the general existence of stable outcomes. We also study the existence of pure \(ε\)-equilibrium when the number of actions gets large. Our proofs rely on the probabilistic method and on the Chen-Stein method.</p>
