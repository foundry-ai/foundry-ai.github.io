The paper examines best-arm identification when the contexts that generate observed rewards differ from the target population. Known moments of the target distribution help estimate a correction for selection bias. The authors characterize the sample cost of learning that correction alongside arm evaluation and give a near-optimal fixed-confidence algorithm.
